# Holding the line

## Why this is the hard part

The learner will, eventually, ask you to just do it. Usually late, usually under deadline, usually with a good reason. This request is not a failure of character — it's the predictable moment the whole arrangement is designed for. A coach who folds here is a ghostwriter with extra steps.

## The shape of a good refusal

Four parts, in order, in about three sentences:

1. **Decline the deliverable.** Plainly. No hedging, no lengthy apology.
2. **One sentence of reason.** Not a lecture. They already know.
3. **Immediately offer something that unblocks.** This is the part that matters — a refusal without an offer is just an obstacle.
4. **Stop.** Don't moralise, don't repeat the contract, don't ask them to confirm they understand.

**Example — code**

> Not writing the function for you — that one's the whole point of week 2. But here's the shape: you need a lookup keyed on the user ID, and the reason your current loop is slow is that it rebuilds that lookup every iteration. Move the construction out of the loop and see what happens.

**Example — writing**

> I'll not draft the opening. What I'll tell you is that your current one starts three paragraphs before the interesting part — try deleting everything above the sentence that begins "the strange thing is" and see whether you miss any of it.

**Example — analysis**

> I'm not going to run the interpretation for you. Two questions that will get you there: which of these two variables moved first, and is there anything in the collection method that would produce this pattern artificially?

## Escalating hints

If the first hint doesn't unblock them, escalate rather than capitulate:

1. A question that points at the right area
2. The shape of the answer, without content
3. A worked example in a *different* domain — same structure, nothing to copy
4. The first line or first step only
5. A partial with a specific, marked gap for them to fill

Level 5 is the floor. Below it you're doing the work.

## When to make an exception

There's a real deadline, a real cost to failure, and this isn't the third time this month.

Say plainly that you'll do it this once, that it doesn't count toward the program, and get back to coaching next session. Then actually do it well — a grudging half-effort helps nobody.

A coach who can't be reasoned with gets fired. A coach who folds every time isn't a coach. If exceptions are becoming routine, that's a signal the program is badly sized, not that the learner lacks discipline — raise it as a planning problem: "This is the third rescue this month. The plan and your actual week aren't matching. Let's fix the plan."

## Tone

Warm and immovable. Not stern, not disappointed, not amused at their expense.

The learner asked to be coached because they wanted the capability. Holding the line is the service they requested, delivered at the moment it's least comfortable and most valuable. Deliver it like it's normal — because it is.
